A calm, material UI system: brushed-metal and stone surfaces, one amber action colour, and a strict state language so players read the position in under a second. Toggle Dark / Light above — everything on this page switches live.

What I found on the current site

Two boards compete

The hero photo contains a 3D hex chess board right behind the real game board. Players can’t tell which one is “the game”.

Text on photo

Body copy and stats sit directly on the castle image; contrast fails in several spots. Cards are all identical, so nothing signals priority.

Toy-like board

Flat saturated green tiles with near-white piece badges give weak tonal hierarchy; highlights would have to fight the tile colour.

Design system

State language (never decorative)

Selected / your actionLegal moveAttack / damageInfo / inactive

Amber is reserved for “you can act now”. Teal = where you can go, red = where damage lands. Allies are round bone-white, enemies are dark rounded-squares, so team is readable by shape as well as colour (colour-blind safe).

Type & surfaces

Barlow Condensed for headings, timers and STR numbers (tight, scoreboard-like). Barlow for UI text. Panels are flat, 1px hairline borders, 8–10px radius; texture only lives on the page background and the board — never behind text.

Contrast: body text ≥ 7:1, amber button text ≥ 7:1 in dark, ≥ 6:1 in light (darker amber). Touch targets ≥ 44px.

Recommended default: Brushed Steel + Slate

Why this pair

Brushed steel is the quietest surface that still feels physical, and its horizontal grain leads the eye to the centred board. Slate tiles are cool mid-greys with tight tonal spread (three tones for hex adjacency), so the bone-white and iron-black pieces get the maximum contrast and amber/teal/red highlights stay unmistakable.

It carries the game’s “fortress” identity (stone + metal) without the castle photo, works equally in dark and light, and keeps the brand amber as the single action colour.

Rules for applying it

1. Texture opacity capped so text never sits on grain.
2. Tile tone difference ≤ 12% luminance; highlights always outrank tile tone.
3. Player-selectable themes must pass an automatic contrast check against both piece colours.
4. Reduced-motion and high-contrast modes drop textures to flat fills.
